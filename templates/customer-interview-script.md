# Customer Interview Script

## Purpose

This script helps you conduct problem-focused customer discovery interviews without pitching too early. The goal is to understand the customer's current reality, not to validate your idea.

Do not show your product or describe your solution during the interview. Listen more than you talk. The most valuable interviews are the ones where the customer surprises you.

Schedule 30–45 minutes. Take notes during or immediately after.

---

## Before the Interview

**Objective**: Learn about the customer's current experience with [problem area]. Understand their workflow, pain points, and what they have tried.

**Mindset**: You are a researcher, not a salesperson. You are not trying to convince them of anything.

**Note-taking**: Use the notes template at the end of this script.

---

## Interview Script

### Opening (2–3 minutes)

"Thank you for making time. I am doing research to better understand how [target role/team] handles [problem area]. I am not here to sell anything today — I am trying to learn from people in your situation.

Would it be okay if I ask you some questions about your current experience? There are no right or wrong answers, and everything you share is confidential."

---

### Problem Discovery (10–12 minutes)

1. "Can you describe your current process for handling [problem area]? Walk me through a typical week."

2. "What is the most frustrating or time-consuming part of that process?"

3. "How often does this come up? Is it a daily problem, weekly, monthly?"

4. "What would happen if this problem was not handled at all? What is the cost — in time, money, or risk?"

5. "How long has it been this way? Has anything made it better or worse recently?"

---

### Workflow Questions (8–10 minutes)

6. "Who else is involved in this process? Who initiates it, who executes it, who reviews it?"

7. "What tools or systems do you currently use for this? What do you like about them? What is missing?"

8. "Have you tried to solve this in a different way before? What happened?"

---

### Buying Process Questions (5 minutes)

9. "If you found a solution that solved this problem, how would you evaluate it? What would you need to see to trust it?"

10. "Who else would need to be involved in approving a new tool or service for this?"

11. "What budget is typically available for solving problems like this?"

---

### Closing (3–5 minutes)

12. "Is there anything about this area that I have not asked about that you think is important?"

13. "Do you know anyone else who deals with this same problem who might be willing to speak with me?"

"Thank you — this has been very helpful. If you are open to it, I may follow up in a few weeks as my research progresses."
