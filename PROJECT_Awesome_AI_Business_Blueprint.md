Recommended tagline:

```text
A step-by-step, anti-hype guide to turn AI use cases into structured offers, validated products, services, and businesses.
```

## 2. Project vision

Awesome AI Business Blueprint is an open-source, educational, and practical repository designed to help builders, consultants, founders, product teams, freelancers, and innovation teams structure AI-powered business opportunities from first principles.

Unlike generic lists of AI tools or “make money with AI” content, this project should focus on the complete business-building process:

```text
Problem → Customer → Use case → Offer → Validation → AI solution → MVP → Pricing → Delivery → Operations → Risk → Growth
```

The project should provide frameworks, templates, examples, checklists, and curated resources that help users design realistic AI-powered businesses.

The repository should be less of a “catalog of ideas” and more of a **business construction manual**.

It should answer:

```text
How do I turn an AI use case into a real business opportunity?
How do I know whether the problem is worth solving?
Who is the buyer?
What should I sell first?
What should the MVP include?
How should I price it?
How should I deliver it?
What risks must I handle?
How can I scale it responsibly?
```

## 3. What this project is

Awesome AI Business Blueprint is:

- A practical business-building guide for AI-powered products and services.
- A structured blueprint for transforming AI use cases into real offers.
- A step-by-step methodology for validating AI business opportunities.
- A template library for customer discovery, offer design, MVP definition, pricing, delivery, and risk assessment.
- A curated knowledge base for AI business models, go-to-market approaches, and operational patterns.
- A sober alternative to misleading AI income claims.
- A GitHub-native handbook that can grow through community contributions.
- A structured dataset that can later power a website, course, assistant, or business design tool.

## 4. What this project is not

This project is not:

- Financial advice.
- Legal advice.
- Tax advice.
- Investment advice.
- A promise of revenue.
- A passive income guide.
- A list of guaranteed AI business ideas.
- A “get rich with AI” resource.
- A low-quality AI side hustle list.
- An affiliate directory.
- A substitute for real customer discovery.
- A substitute for legal, compliance, privacy, or security review.

Every blueprint, framework, business model, and pricing suggestion should be treated as a hypothesis that requires validation.

## 5. Core positioning

The repository should be positioned around this statement:

```text
A practical blueprint for designing, validating, and building AI-powered businesses — without hype or income promises.
```

The word “blueprint” is important. It implies:

- Structure.
- Method.
- Construction.
- Completeness.
- Repeatability.
- Clarity.
- Step-by-step execution.

The repository should attract people who want to build seriously, not people looking for shortcuts.

## 6. Target audiences

### 6.1 Serious beginners

People who are interested in AI business opportunities but need a structured method to think clearly.

### 6.2 Indie builders

People building micro-SaaS, AI tools, browser extensions, automations, templates, or small products.

### 6.3 Freelancers and consultants

People who want to package AI expertise into clear service offers, implementation packages, training programs, or retainers.

### 6.4 Startup founders

People who want to validate AI-native business ideas, define their MVP, and structure a credible go-to-market plan.

### 6.5 Product managers and innovation teams

People inside companies who need to evaluate internal AI use cases, build business cases, and define implementation roadmaps.

### 6.6 Educators, mentors, and community builders

People teaching AI entrepreneurship, productization, and responsible business design.

## 7. Editorial principles

The repository must prioritize clarity, usefulness, and responsible business thinking.

### 7.1 Required principles

Every contribution should be:

- Practical.
- Specific.
- Educational.
- Honest.
- Internationally understandable.
- Actionable.
- Structured.
- Clear about uncertainty.
- Clear about assumptions.
- Clear about validation requirements.
- Clear about risks and limitations.

### 7.2 Forbidden editorial patterns

Do not publish content that uses or implies:

- Guaranteed revenue.
- Passive income without effort.
- Easy money.
- “Anyone can make $10k/month.”
- Fake urgency.
- Fake scarcity.
- Fake testimonials.
- Unsupported revenue claims.
- Manipulative marketing.
- Spam automation.
- Deceptive AI content.
- Unauthorized scraping.
- Deepfake scams.
- Phishing or impersonation.
- Privacy-invasive monitoring.
- High-stakes automated decisions without human oversight.

### 7.3 Preferred language

Prefer:

```text
Business hypothesis

Validation plan

Pricing hypothesis
```

Avoid:

## 9. Core modules

### 9.1 Blueprint

Purpose:

```text
Provide a step-by-step methodology to design, validate, build, and operate an AI-powered business.
```

The Blueprint module is the heart of the repository. It should be written like a practical curriculum.

Each chapter should include:

- Objective
- Why it matters
- Key concepts
- Step-by-step process
- Questions to answer
- Common mistakes
- Deliverables
- Templates
- Example
- Checklist

### 9.2 Canvases

Purpose:

```text
Provide one-page strategic tools to structure thinking.
```

Canvases should be lightweight and reusable.

Required canvases:

- AI Business Canvas
- AI Use Case Canvas
- AI Offer Canvas
- AI MVP Canvas
- AI Risk Canvas
- AI Go-to-Market Canvas
- AI Operating Model Canvas

### 9.3 Templates

Purpose:

```text
Help users execute each step of the blueprint.
```

Templates should be copyable, practical, and easy to complete.

Examples:

- Customer interview script
- Problem validation template
- Competitor analysis template
- MVP specification template
- Pricing worksheet
- Pilot project plan
- Delivery checklist
- Risk assessment template

### 9.4 Business Models

Purpose:

```text
Explain realistic AI business models and how to apply them.
```

Each business model page should include:

- Description
- Best-fit situations
- Buyer types
- Revenue mechanics
- Pricing patterns
- Delivery requirements
- Cost structure
- Risks
- Examples
- Validation questions
- Common mistakes

### 9.5 Examples

Purpose:

```text
Show how the blueprint applies to concrete AI business opportunities.
```

Examples should not be shallow ideas. Each example should walk through:

- Problem
- Customer
- Offer
- MVP
- Technical stack
- Pricing hypothesis
- Validation plan
- Delivery model
- Risks
- Scaling path

### 9.6 Case Studies

Purpose:

```text
Document real or public examples of AI products, services, and companies when reliable sources are available.
```

Case studies should be evidence-based and avoid unsupported revenue claims.

### 9.7 Resources

Purpose:

```text
Curate educational, technical, business, and operational resources.
```

Resources should support the blueprint steps, not merely list random AI tools.

## 15. Validation scoring framework

Each business example should be evaluated using practical dimensions.

```yaml
scoring_dimensions:
  pain_intensity:
    description: "How painful, frequent, and costly is the problem?"
    scale: "1 = weak, 5 = strong"

  willingness_to_pay:
    description: "Is there evidence that the target customer may pay?"
    scale: "1 = unlikely, 5 = very likely"

  ai_fit:
    description: "Does AI meaningfully improve the workflow?"
    scale: "1 = weak fit, 5 = strong fit"

  implementation_complexity:
    description: "How hard is it to build and deliver?"
    scale: "1 = simple, 5 = very complex"

  distribution_difficulty:
    description: "How hard is it to reach the target customer?"
    scale: "1 = easy, 5 = very hard"

  compliance_risk:
    description: "Are there legal, privacy, ethical, security, or regulatory risks?"
    scale: "1 = low, 5 = high"

  operational_complexity:
    description: "How hard is it to onboard, maintain, support, and scale?"
    scale: "1 = simple, 5 = very complex"

  differentiation_potential:
    description: "Can the offer become defensible through workflow, data, trust, domain expertise, or integrations?"
    scale: "1 = weak, 5 = strong"
```

A possible prioritization score:

```text
blueprint_priority =
  + 2.0 * pain_intensity
  + 1.8 * willingness_to_pay
  + 1.5 * ai_fit
  + 1.2 * differentiation_potential
  - 1.0 * implementation_complexity
  - 1.0 * distribution_difficulty
  - 0.8 * compliance_risk
  - 0.8 * operational_complexity
```

This score must be described as a prioritization aid, not a profitability prediction.

### 19.4 Update pipeline flow

```text
Discover → Normalize → Deduplicate → Classify → Score → Generate candidates → Human review → Merge
```

Detailed flow:

1. Read configured sources from `data/sources.yaml`.
2. Fetch recent entries.
3. Normalize title, URL, description, source, tags, and metadata.
4. Deduplicate entries by canonical URL, normalized title, and repository name.
5. Classify into:
   - resource_candidate
   - example_candidate
   - business_model_reference
   - template_reference
   - case_study_candidate
   - tool_reference
6. Score by relevance and usefulness.
7. Write outputs to:
   - `generated/latest-discoveries.md`
   - `generated/pending-review.md`
   - `generated/weekly-update-report.md`
   - `data/updates_log.yaml`
8. Open a pull request for maintainers to review.
9. Require human approval before adding to accepted curated files.

## 25. Contribution guidelines

`CONTRIBUTING.md` should include:

### 25.1 Accepted contributions

- Blueprint improvements.
- Templates.
- Canvases.
- Business model explanations.
- Business examples.
- Case studies with sources.
- Resource suggestions.
- Risk and compliance improvements.
- Corrections and broken link fixes.

### 25.2 Quality requirements

Contributions must be:

- Practical.
- Specific.
- Educational.
- Internationally understandable.
- Clear about assumptions.
- Free from revenue guarantees.
- Free from spam or affiliate bias.
- Clear about risks where relevant.
- Supported by sources when factual claims are made.

### 25.3 Rejection reasons

A contribution may be rejected if it is:

- Too vague.
- Too promotional.
- Misleading.
- Duplicated.
- Unsupported by sources.
- Ethically problematic.
- Legally risky.
- Mostly affiliate content.
- Based on unrealistic claims.
- Not related to AI business building.

### 25.4 Sensitive domains

For healthcare, finance, insurance, legal, employment, education evaluation, public services, security, and law enforcement, include:

```text
This use case may involve regulated or high-stakes decisions. Human review, compliance assessment, security controls, privacy review, and domain expertise are required before production use.
```
