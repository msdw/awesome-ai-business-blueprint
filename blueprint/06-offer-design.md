# Step 6 — Offer Design

## Overview

Offer design is the step where you translate the use case and business model into a specific, bounded, and communicable product or service that a buyer can evaluate and purchase. A well-designed offer removes ambiguity from the buyer's perspective and sets clear expectations for what is delivered, at what price, over what timeline.

Most AI ventures fail not because the technology is wrong but because the offer is unclear. Buyers need to understand exactly what they are buying, what they are not buying, what success looks like, and what it costs. Vague offers create hesitation, long sales cycles, and scope disputes.

## Key Questions to Answer

- What exactly is included in this offer?
- What is explicitly excluded?
- Who is the specific buyer for this offer?
- What does success look like from the buyer's perspective?
- How is the value of the offer communicated in buyer-friendly language?
- What proof would a skeptical buyer need to say yes?
- What are the three pricing options or tiers?

## Core Activities

1. **Write the offer description**: Describe what the buyer receives in plain language. Avoid technical jargon.
2. **Define scope**: List what is included, what is excluded, and any prerequisites.
3. **Define deliverables**: Specify the concrete outputs the buyer will receive (a configured system, a weekly report, a trained model, an API endpoint, a consulting engagement).
4. **Write success criteria**: Define what "done" and "successful" mean in measurable terms.
5. **Define pricing tiers**: Create 2–3 options that allow the buyer to self-select based on their needs and budget.
6. **Identify proof requirements**: What evidence would a skeptical buyer need? Case studies, a live demo, a pilot project, a reference customer?
7. **Review the offer for guarantees to avoid**: Do not promise specific outcomes (revenue, performance, accuracy) that you cannot control.

## Common Mistakes

- **Offers that are too broad**: "We help companies with AI" is not an offer. "We configure a document extraction pipeline for your invoice processing team in 4 weeks" is an offer.
- **Not defining what is not included**: Scope creep starts when exclusions are not written down before the engagement begins.
- **Copying competitor pricing without understanding your cost structure**: Your delivery costs may be different. Price based on your economics, not on what you think the market expects.
- **Avoiding a pilot or proof structure**: Buyers who are skeptical need a low-risk entry point. A paid pilot reduces risk for both sides.
- **Using AI marketing language in the offer**: Avoid terms like "AI-powered," "revolutionary," or "cutting-edge." Use outcome language: "reduces processing time," "eliminates manual re-entry."

## Deliverables

- **Offer description**: A 200-word summary of what the buyer receives, written in buyer-friendly language.
- **Target buyer profile**: A description of the specific person or team who would buy this offer.
- **Scope document**: A list of included items, excluded items, and prerequisites.
- **Deliverables list**: The concrete outputs the buyer receives.
- **Success criteria**: Measurable definition of a successful engagement.
- **Pricing tiers**: 2–3 options with prices and differences clearly stated.
- **Proof plan**: The evidence or demonstration structure needed to support the sales process.
