# Step 10 — Go-to-Market

## Overview

Go-to-market (GTM) is the plan for reaching and converting the first 10 paying customers. It is not a marketing strategy or a growth plan — it is a focused 30-to-60-day sequence of specific actions designed to generate initial revenue and learning.

Most early-stage AI ventures do not fail because the product is wrong. They fail because the builder never systematically pursued the first customers. GTM at the early stage is almost always a manual, direct effort: outreach, referrals, communities, and direct conversations.

## Key Questions to Answer

- Where do the target buyers spend their time online and offline?
- What message resonates based on what you learned in customer discovery?
- What is the conversion path from first contact to signed agreement?
- What proof points can you offer to reduce buyer risk?
- Who are the first 20 specific people or organizations you will contact?
- What does a successful first 30 days look like?

## Core Activities

1. **Define the positioning statement**: Write one sentence that identifies who the offer is for, what it does, and why it is different from alternatives.
2. **Select 1–2 primary channels**: Choose the channels most likely to reach your target buyer. For B2B, this is usually direct outreach (LinkedIn, email) or communities. For developers, it may be content, GitHub, or forums.
3. **Build the first 20 prospect list**: Name 20 specific organizations or individuals you will contact in the first two weeks. Generic targeting is not a plan.
4. **Draft the outreach message**: Write a short, direct message that acknowledges the buyer's problem and offers a specific, low-risk next step (a 20-minute call, not a demo or a proposal).
5. **Create proof assets**: Prepare the minimum proof required to support the sales conversation: a one-pager, a brief case study, a live demo, or a short explainer video.
6. **Define the first conversion milestone**: Set a specific, observable milestone for the first 30 days (3 discovery calls, 1 pilot agreement, 1 paying customer).

## Common Mistakes

- **Building a product without a GTM plan**: The product is not the GTM. Many builders ship and wait. First customers almost never find you passively at the early stage.
- **Relying on a Product Hunt launch as the primary GTM**: Launch events generate spikes of attention, not sustained customer acquisition. They are marketing tactics, not GTM strategies.
- **Using vague outreach**: "I built an AI tool, interested?" will not convert. Be specific about the problem, the outcome, and the ask.
- **Not defining a conversion path**: Buyers need to know what happens after the first conversation. Define each step: discovery call → demo → pilot agreement → payment.
- **Trying to reach everyone**: A focused effort on 20 specific, well-chosen prospects is more effective than broadcasting to thousands.

## Deliverables

- **Positioning statement**: One sentence describing who the offer is for, what it does, and why it is different.
- **Channel selection**: The 1–2 channels you will use with rationale.
- **First 20 prospects**: A named or clearly described list of initial outreach targets.
- **Outreach message draft**: The first message you will send to prospects.
- **Proof assets**: The materials that support the sales conversation.
- **30-day conversion milestone**: A specific, measurable outcome for the first 30 days.
