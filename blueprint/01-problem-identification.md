# Step 1 — Problem Identification

## Overview

The first step of the AI Business Blueprint is identifying a specific, painful, and observable problem that is worth solving. Most failed AI ventures start from a technology or a tool rather than from a problem. The core discipline here is resisting the urge to build until you have clearly defined what the pain is, who feels it, and what it costs.

A good problem is frequent, costly, and currently handled in a way that is clearly inadequate. The goal of this step is to produce a problem statement that can be tested with real potential customers.

## Key Questions to Answer

- Who has this problem and how frequently do they encounter it?
- What does solving or not solving the problem cost them in time, money, or risk?
- How are they currently handling the problem?
- Is there evidence of the problem in public forums, job posts, support tickets, or reviews?
- Is AI a plausible fit, or would a simpler solution work?
- Who owns the budget for solving this problem?

## Core Activities

1. **Identify problem candidates**: List 5–10 potential problems in a domain you know. Do not filter yet.
2. **Score each problem**: Rate each candidate on frequency (how often), severity (how painful), and economic value (what it costs or saves).
3. **Search for evidence**: Look for forum threads, Reddit discussions, job postings, product reviews, and LinkedIn posts that mention the problem.
4. **Write a problem statement**: Describe the problem in one paragraph, naming the user, the situation, what goes wrong, and what it costs.
5. **Identify candidate customer segments**: List 2–3 types of organizations or individuals who are likely to face this problem.

## Common Mistakes

- **Starting from a tool**: Saying "I want to build something with GPT-4" is not a problem statement. Find the pain first.
- **Choosing an interesting problem over a painful one**: Academic or intellectually interesting problems may have no paying buyers.
- **Ignoring who controls the budget**: Technical users often feel the pain but do not hold purchasing authority.
- **Defining the problem too broadly**: "Businesses need AI" is not a problem. "B2B sales teams spend 4 hours per week writing follow-up emails manually" is a problem.
- **Skipping evidence collection**: Assumptions without evidence are just opinions. Search for at least 3 independent signals before proceeding.

## Deliverables

- **Problem statement**: One paragraph naming the user, the situation, the failure mode, and the cost.
- **Target user profile**: 3–5 bullet points describing the person who experiences this problem daily.
- **Evidence of the problem**: At least 3 independent sources (forum posts, job listings, analyst reports, interview notes).
- **Problem score**: A simple 1–10 rating on frequency, severity, and economic value for each candidate problem.
- **Initial customer segment hypothesis**: 2–3 potential customer segments to explore in the next step.
