# Step 2 — Market Validation

## Overview

Market validation is the process of verifying that a sufficient number of potential paying buyers exist before committing to building. This step prevents the common mistake of building a product for a market that is too small, too competitive, or not ready to pay.

Validation does not mean certainty. It means reducing the most dangerous assumptions to an acceptable level of risk. At this stage, you are not selling — you are gathering evidence that demand exists and that the market is reachable.

## Key Questions to Answer

- How many organizations or individuals have this problem in the markets you can realistically reach?
- Is the market growing, stable, or declining?
- Who are the existing competitors, and what does their traction tell you about demand?
- What are people currently paying to solve this problem (directly or indirectly)?
- Where do potential buyers search for solutions?
- Is the problem consistently mentioned in public communities, job postings, or product reviews?

## Core Activities

1. **Estimate addressable market size**: Use bottom-up reasoning rather than top-down TAM figures. Count the specific organizations or roles that have the problem and can be reached.
2. **Map competitors**: Find 3–5 direct and adjacent competitors. Note their pricing, positioning, customer base, and reviews. Competitors are validation — if none exist, question whether the problem is real.
3. **Collect market signals**: Search Google Trends, Reddit, LinkedIn, job boards, and product marketplaces for terms related to the problem. Volume is not the goal — pattern recognition is.
4. **Identify pricing signals**: Look at competitor pricing pages, freelancer rates on Upwork/Fiverr, and any visible contract sizes to calibrate willingness to pay.
5. **Make a go/no-go recommendation**: Based on the evidence, decide whether the market is worth moving forward into customer discovery.

## Common Mistakes

- **Using TAM without reachability analysis**: A billion-dollar market means nothing if you cannot reach a buyer within 90 days.
- **Dismissing competitors**: Strong competitors confirm demand. Weak or no competitors should raise questions about market readiness.
- **Confusing search volume with buying intent**: People searching for information are not the same as people ready to pay.
- **Validating with friends and family**: Biased sources produce biased signals. Use public forums, real reviews, and strangers.
- **Skipping the pricing signal step**: Builders who never check what buyers currently pay often price too low or too high later.

## Deliverables

- **Market size estimate**: A bottom-up calculation of the number of reachable potential buyers, with source.
- **Competitor map**: 3–5 competitors with notes on their pricing, positioning, and visible traction.
- **Market signal evidence**: Screenshots, links, or summaries from at least 3 independent public sources.
- **Pricing signals**: A range of what buyers currently pay for adjacent solutions.
- **Go/no-go decision**: A written recommendation to proceed, pause, or pivot, with rationale.
