# Step 11 — Distribution Channels

## Overview

Distribution channels are the paths through which customers discover, evaluate, and purchase your product or service. After the initial GTM effort (Step 10), this step focuses on identifying and investing in the channels that will drive sustainable, scalable customer acquisition over time.

Finding a channel that works is one of the hardest and most important tasks in building an AI business. Most channels require significant time before showing results. The goal of this step is to identify the highest-potential channels for your specific buyer type and price point, run small experiments, and double down on what works.

## Key Questions to Answer

- Which channels are most used by buyers at your price point and buying behavior?
- What is the cost and expected time-to-first-customer for each candidate channel?
- Which channel can scale without proportional cost increases?
- What content or positioning assets do you need to make each channel work?
- Which channels can be tested within 30 days with minimal investment?
- What does a positive signal look like for each channel experiment?

## Core Activities

1. **List candidate channels**: Map all realistic channels for your buyer type. Common options include direct outbound, referrals, content marketing, SEO, paid advertising, partnerships, communities, marketplace listings, developer ecosystems, and event-based selling.
2. **Prioritize by buyer match**: Rank channels by how well they match your target buyer's discovery and evaluation habits. B2B enterprise buyers respond to direct outreach and referrals. SMBs often use search. Developers use GitHub and forums.
3. **Estimate channel economics**: For each candidate channel, estimate the cost per lead, time to first customer, and potential scale.
4. **Run one experiment per channel**: Design a minimal, time-boxed test for each of your top 2–3 channels. Define the success metric before running the test.
5. **Identify the winner**: After 30–60 days of channel experiments, double down on the channel with the clearest signal of buyer interest and conversion.
6. **Build channel-specific assets**: Develop the content, landing pages, messaging, or partnerships required to operate the winning channel at scale.

## Common Mistakes

- **Spreading too thin across channels**: Testing 6 channels simultaneously produces no useful signal. Start with 1–2.
- **Ignoring outbound for high-value B2B offers**: Organic discovery takes months. For offers above $5,000, direct outreach is often the fastest path to first revenue.
- **Treating content marketing as a near-term GTM strategy**: Content generates compounding returns over 6–18 months. It is not a channel for the first 60 days of a new product.
- **Conflating attention with acquisition**: Social media followers and newsletter subscribers are not customers. Measure conversion, not engagement.
- **Not defining success before running experiments**: Without a pre-defined success metric, every channel looks equally promising or equally disappointing.

## Deliverables

- **Channel priority list**: The top 3 channels with rationale for each.
- **Channel experiment plan**: A time-boxed test for each channel with a clear success metric.
- **Channel economics estimate**: Cost per lead and expected time-to-first-customer for each channel.
- **Channel-specific assets**: The content, pages, or tools required to operate each channel.
