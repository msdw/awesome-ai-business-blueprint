# Step 3 — Customer Discovery

## Overview

Customer discovery is the practice of talking directly to potential customers before building anything. It is the most reliable way to understand whether the problem is real, how painful it is, what the current workaround looks like, and whether someone would pay for a better solution.

This step is not about pitching your idea. It is about listening. The goal is to gather evidence from real people about their actual behavior, not their hypothetical preferences.

Good customer discovery interviews produce insights that you could not have guessed from a desk: the exact language buyers use, the workarounds they have cobbled together, the decision-making process, the true cost of the problem, and the real objections to buying.

## Key Questions to Answer

- What is the customer's current workflow for handling this problem?
- How much time or money does the problem cost them on a recurring basis?
- What have they already tried to solve it? Why did previous attempts fail?
- Who makes the purchasing decision and what does their approval process look like?
- What would have to be true for them to consider a new solution?
- What are the top 3 objections they would have to buying?

## Core Activities

1. **Identify interview targets**: Find 10–15 people who match your customer segment hypothesis. Use LinkedIn, communities, warm introductions, or cold outreach.
2. **Schedule 30-minute conversations**: Frame the ask as a research conversation, not a sales call.
3. **Conduct problem-focused interviews**: Use open-ended questions. Ask about the past, not about hypotheticals. Never pitch during the interview.
4. **Take structured notes**: Capture quotes, surprising statements, workflow details, and emotional signals.
5. **Synthesize findings**: After 5–10 interviews, identify the top 3 pain themes, the most common workarounds, and the clearest signals of willingness to pay.
6. **Update your problem statement**: Revise the problem statement from Step 1 based on what you heard.

## Common Mistakes

- **Asking hypothetical questions**: "Would you use this?" produces meaningless answers. Ask "Tell me about the last time you had this problem."
- **Pitching during the interview**: Once you start selling, the customer starts being polite instead of honest.
- **Only talking to people who like your idea**: Seek out skeptics and people who have tried alternatives.
- **Treating a small sample as definitive**: 5 interviews reveal patterns; they do not confirm a market.
- **Skipping this step entirely**: Building without customer discovery is the highest-risk path. Even one week of interviews reduces error dramatically.
- **Ignoring the buying process**: Understanding who approves the purchase is as important as understanding who uses the product.

## Deliverables

- **5–10 customer interview notes**: Structured summaries of each conversation including key quotes and workflow details.
- **Key insight summary**: The top 3 pain themes identified across interviews, with supporting quotes.
- **Buying process map**: A description of who initiates, evaluates, approves, and pays for solutions in the target segment.
- **Revised problem statement**: An updated version of the Step 1 problem statement, refined by real customer language.
- **Willingness-to-pay evidence**: Specific signals from interviews about budget, current spend, and openness to paying.
