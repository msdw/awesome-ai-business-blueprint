# Step 15 — Scaling

## Overview

Scaling is the step where you systematically grow the business after demonstrating that the core product-market fit exists, delivery is reliable, and unit economics are positive. The most important principle of this step is that scaling before these conditions are met accelerates problems rather than solving them.

Scaling an AI business involves more than growing revenue. It requires standardizing delivery processes, automating repeatable tasks, building team capabilities, deepening integrations, and preparing systems for higher volume without proportional cost increases.

## Key Questions to Answer

- What evidence suggests product-market fit before committing to scaling?
- What is the unit economics target before hiring, raising, or investing significantly in acquisition?
- What are the primary bottlenecks to growth? (delivery capacity, sales process, product gaps, churn)
- What does scaling look like specifically for this business model?
- What can be standardized and documented to reduce founder dependency?
- What partnerships or integrations would significantly accelerate distribution?

## Core Activities

1. **Assess product-market fit**: Check for clear retention, organic referrals, and customer willingness to pay at higher price points. Do not assume product-market fit from early traction alone.
2. **Audit unit economics**: Confirm that the gross margin is positive and sustainable at scale. Identify the cost drivers that will change as volume grows.
3. **Document and standardize processes**: Write down every delivery, onboarding, support, and quality control process. Identify what can be templated, automated, or delegated.
4. **Identify hiring or outsourcing needs**: Determine which capacity constraints require additional people and which can be resolved through automation or better tooling.
5. **Evaluate partnership and integration opportunities**: Identify the distribution partners, technology integrations, or ecosystem relationships that could accelerate acquisition or retention.
6. **Build a 12-month growth plan**: Define revenue targets, acquisition milestones, product investments, and team growth with realistic assumptions.

## Common Mistakes

- **Scaling channels before fixing retention**: Acquiring customers faster than you retain them creates a growth illusion and an operational crisis.
- **Hiring before validating repeatable sales**: The first few salespeople will not save a broken sales motion. Validate the sales process manually before hiring to replicate it.
- **Underestimating operational complexity at scale**: What works with 5 customers often breaks at 50. Plan for operational complexity before it becomes an emergency.
- **Skipping documentation**: Founder-dependent processes cannot be delegated. Documentation is not optional for scaling.
- **Scaling a product with known retention problems**: Churn that is acceptable at 10 customers becomes an existential threat at 500. Fix retention before scaling.

## Deliverables

- **Product-market fit assessment**: A structured evaluation of the evidence for product-market fit.
- **Unit economics review**: Updated calculation of revenue per customer, cost per customer, and gross margin.
- **Standard operating procedures**: Written processes for delivery, onboarding, quality review, and support.
- **12-month growth plan**: Revenue targets, acquisition milestones, team growth, and product investments.
- **Scaling readiness scorecard**: A structured checklist of the conditions that must be true before committing to significant scaling investment.
