# Step 5 — Business Model Selection

## Overview

Business model selection is the step where you decide how the value you deliver will be packaged and monetized. The choice of business model affects everything downstream: pricing, delivery, operations, customer relationships, and scalability.

AI enables a broader set of business models than traditional software. The same AI capability can be delivered as a self-serve SaaS product, a productized service, an implementation package, a consulting engagement, a maintenance retainer, or a paid template. The right choice depends on the buyer context, the value delivered, the delivery complexity, and the stage of the business.

## Key Questions to Answer

- How does the target buyer measure value from this solution? Per time saved, per output generated, per risk avoided, or per revenue enabled?
- What pricing model is the buyer already familiar with in adjacent products?
- What is the cost to deliver this solution relative to the price?
- Which model allows for the fastest path to first revenue and iteration?
- Does the buyer prefer to own the solution, subscribe to it, or outsource it?
- What level of customization does the buyer expect?

## Core Activities

1. **Review the business model options**: Subscription SaaS, usage-based API, productized service, custom consulting, implementation package, maintenance retainer, paid template, paid workflow, training course, marketplace product, enterprise license, or internal tooling.
2. **Match model to buyer context**: Enterprise buyers often prefer implementation packages or licenses. SMBs prefer fixed-price services or subscriptions. Developers prefer usage-based APIs.
3. **Estimate unit economics**: Calculate the cost of delivery per customer or per transaction. Ensure the pricing model can sustain a margin above costs.
4. **Choose a model for the MVP**: Select the model that minimizes delivery complexity and maximizes speed to first paying customer.
5. **Draft a pricing hypothesis**: Define a price range, not an exact number. The hypothesis will be refined in Step 9.

## Common Mistakes

- **Defaulting to SaaS without testing buyer willingness**: SaaS scales well but requires more trust before first payment. A service contract is often faster for early validation.
- **Setting prices too low to be taken seriously**: Prices signal quality. Underpricing can reduce perceived value and make the economics unworkable.
- **Choosing a model that requires too much trust before first payment**: Enterprise procurement can take 6–12 months. A pilot project or small engagement can accelerate the relationship.
- **Ignoring delivery costs**: AI solutions have ongoing inference costs, maintenance overhead, and support requirements that must be reflected in the pricing model.
- **Confusing revenue model with business model**: Recurring revenue is a metric. A business model defines who pays, for what, how often, and how value is delivered.

## Deliverables

- **Selected business model**: The chosen model with a clear rationale.
- **Pricing hypothesis**: A realistic price range for the initial offer, with notes on how it was derived.
- **Unit economics sketch**: A rough calculation of revenue per customer, cost per customer, and gross margin estimate.
- **Alternative models considered**: A brief note on what other models were evaluated and why they were not selected.
