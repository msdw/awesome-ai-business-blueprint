# Step 12 — Delivery and Operations

## Overview

Delivery and operations covers the processes, systems, and standards required to deliver your product or service reliably and profitably. It is the step where the business moves from selling to consistently executing.

Many AI businesses handle their first few customers through heroic, unstructured effort. This works at very small scale but breaks as volume grows. The goal of this step is to define a repeatable delivery process that can be executed by others, maintained over time, and improved systematically.

## Key Questions to Answer

- What is the delivery process step by step, from signed agreement to delivered value?
- What can be automated versus what requires human judgment?
- What are the quality gates? How will errors or substandard outputs be detected before reaching the customer?
- What is the cost of delivery per customer, including time, infrastructure, and support?
- How will customers be onboarded? What do they need to do in the first 7 days?
- What does the ongoing support model look like?

## Core Activities

1. **Map the delivery process**: Write out every step from customer agreement to delivered value. Include data collection, access setup, configuration, testing, training, go-live, and monitoring.
2. **Identify human review requirements**: Mark every step where AI output must be reviewed by a human before it reaches the customer. This is especially critical in regulated or high-stakes contexts.
3. **Build the onboarding sequence**: Define what the customer needs to do to get started, what you need from them, and how long onboarding should take.
4. **Establish quality standards**: Define what good output looks like. Create checklists that delivery team members use before releasing work to customers.
5. **Calculate delivery cost**: Time the delivery process and calculate the cost per customer including labor, infrastructure, and tools.
6. **Define the support model**: Specify what support is included, response time expectations, escalation paths, and what is out of scope.
7. **Plan for maintenance**: Define how the AI system will be monitored, updated, and maintained after delivery.

## Common Mistakes

- **Not defining quality standards before scaling**: Without written quality criteria, output quality degrades as volume grows.
- **Underpricing because delivery costs were not calculated**: Many AI service providers discover negative margins after signing their first customers.
- **No human review step for AI outputs in high-stakes contexts**: Skipping human review in domains with significant consequences is an operational and reputational risk.
- **Manual processes that cannot scale**: Delivery processes that require constant founder involvement create a ceiling on growth.
- **No monitoring after go-live**: AI systems degrade over time as data and context change. Monitoring is not optional.

## Deliverables

- **Delivery process map**: A step-by-step description of the full delivery workflow.
- **Onboarding checklist**: The tasks required to onboard a new customer successfully.
- **Quality standards document**: Written criteria for acceptable output at each stage.
- **Support model definition**: What support is included, at what response time, and through what channel.
- **Delivery cost calculation**: The all-in cost to deliver per customer, updated to reflect real experience.
- **Maintenance plan**: How the system will be monitored and updated after go-live.
