# Step 14 — Growth and Iteration

## Overview

Growth and iteration is the step where you establish the feedback loop and measurement system that allows you to improve the product and grow revenue based on real usage. This step prevents the common pattern of building features based on assumptions rather than evidence.

At this stage, the goal is not to grow fast — it is to understand what is working, why, and what to improve. Sustainable growth follows retention. The most important metric at this stage is not new customers acquired but whether existing customers continue to use the product and whether they would recommend it.

## Key Questions to Answer

- What is the one metric that best indicates whether the product is delivering real value?
- What is the current retention rate, and what is the target?
- What feedback are customers giving, and how is it being collected systematically?
- What is the weekly iteration cycle for improving the product?
- Which channels are showing early signals worth investing in further?
- What are the top 3 priorities from user feedback?

## Core Activities

1. **Define the north star metric**: Choose one metric that best captures the core value delivered to customers. This should be an outcome metric, not a vanity metric.
2. **Set up a metrics dashboard**: Track 3–5 key metrics weekly: retention, usage frequency, customer satisfaction, revenue growth, and cost of delivery.
3. **Build a feedback collection process**: Establish a regular cadence for collecting customer feedback — weekly check-ins for new customers, monthly reviews for established ones.
4. **Define the iteration cycle**: Set a weekly or biweekly rhythm for reviewing feedback, prioritizing improvements, and shipping changes.
5. **Build an experiment log**: Record every product change, the hypothesis behind it, and the result. This builds institutional knowledge and prevents repeating mistakes.
6. **Identify growth levers**: Based on the data, identify the 1–2 changes most likely to improve retention or accelerate acquisition.

## Common Mistakes

- **Tracking too many metrics without a north star**: When everything is measured, nothing is prioritized. Choose one leading metric that matters most.
- **Optimizing acquisition before fixing retention**: Bringing in new customers while losing existing ones is an expensive way to stand still.
- **No systematic feedback collection process**: Anecdotal feedback leads to building for the loudest voice rather than the most representative one.
- **Iterating without logging changes**: Without a record of what changed and why, it is impossible to know what caused improvements or regressions.
- **Treating growth as a marketing problem**: Most early growth comes from product improvements, referrals, and word of mouth. Fix the product before investing in acquisition.

## Deliverables

- **North star metric**: The one metric that best captures product value, with current baseline.
- **Metrics dashboard definition**: 3–5 key metrics tracked weekly, with data sources and reporting cadence.
- **Feedback collection process**: How customer feedback is collected, recorded, and reviewed.
- **Iteration backlog**: The top 3 improvement priorities derived from user feedback.
- **Experiment log**: A record of product changes, hypotheses, and outcomes.
