# Step 9 — Pricing Strategy

## Overview

Pricing strategy is the step where you set a price that reflects the value you deliver, the cost to deliver it, and the buyer's willingness to pay. Pricing is not a final decision at this stage — it is a hypothesis that will be tested and refined through real sales conversations.

Pricing an AI product or service requires balancing three variables: the value delivered to the buyer (the ceiling), the cost to deliver including inference, support, and overhead (the floor), and the competitive context (the reference). The goal is to find a price that creates a clear return on investment for the buyer while sustaining a viable margin for you.

## Key Questions to Answer

- What is the measurable ROI for the buyer at the proposed price?
- What are comparable products and services priced at?
- What price signals quality and commitment to the buyer?
- What is the cost to deliver — including LLM inference, hosting, support, and your own time?
- What is the gross margin at the proposed price?
- How will the price evolve as the product matures and gains credibility?

## Core Activities

1. **Calculate the value delivered**: Estimate the time saved, cost avoided, or revenue enabled for the buyer at the proposed price. Price should be a fraction of this value.
2. **Calculate the delivery cost**: Sum all variable costs (inference, infrastructure, third-party tools) and a portion of fixed costs per customer.
3. **Review competitor pricing**: Note pricing pages for 3–5 comparable products. Use this as a reference range, not a ceiling.
4. **Design the packaging structure**: Decide between flat pricing, tiered pricing, usage-based pricing, or a combination.
5. **Test the price in conversations**: Before finalizing, mention the price in sales conversations and observe reactions. Adjust based on what you learn.
6. **Define a price increase plan**: Set a milestone (e.g., 10 paying customers) after which you will raise prices to test the upper bound of willingness to pay.

## Common Mistakes

- **Underpricing to attract early users**: Low prices attract price-sensitive customers who are harder to retain and who provide less useful feedback. Price for the buyer you want.
- **Setting prices without knowing delivery costs**: Many AI products have negative margins at early pricing because inference costs were not calculated.
- **Charging per seat without testing value-based alternatives**: Seat-based pricing punishes adoption. Consider outcome-based or usage-based alternatives.
- **Not building in a price increase plan**: If you launch at a price you cannot raise, you have anchored the market at an unsustainable level.
- **Treating competitor prices as the ceiling**: Your value delivery may be significantly higher or lower than competitors. Price based on your economics.

## Deliverables

- **Pricing hypothesis**: The proposed price or price range with rationale.
- **Package structure**: The tiers or options available to buyers.
- **Delivery cost model**: A calculation of the cost to deliver per customer or per unit.
- **Gross margin estimate**: The margin at the proposed price and cost structure.
- **Pricing test plan**: How you will test and validate the price before finalizing it.
